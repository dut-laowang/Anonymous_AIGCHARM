Annotation Manual

Guidelines for harmfulness, category, and explicitness labels

This page summarizes the annotation criteria used for AIGenHarm-Video in a reviewer-friendly format.

01

Harmful Video Definition

A video is labeled harmful when visual, audio, textual, or cross-frame evidence presents, induces, promotes, or disseminates safety risks under platform community-safety policies.

Covered risks include hate or harassment, violence or dangerous acts, sexual content, child-safety risks, illegal activity or regulated goods, and misinformation or political sensitivity.

02

Explicit Harm

Explicit harm is assigned when the risk is directly observable or directly interpretable: clear violence, nudity, weapon use, illegal instructions, direct insults or threats, explicit false claims, or direct child-safety risks.

03

Implicit Harm

Implicit harm is assigned when harmfulness mainly emerges from indirect narrative, metaphor, irony, suggestive composition, anthropomorphic character relations, or platform context rather than a single explicit visual cue.

Implicit Criteria

When a harmful sample is treated as implicit

C1

Hate / Harassment

Metaphor, stereotypes, derogatory narration, or suggestive language without direct violent imagery.

C2

Violence / Dangerous Acts

Low-intensity simulated conflict, playful fighting, or scenes whose harmful reading depends on context.

C3

Sexual Content

Sexual risk conveyed through language, posture, objects, or suggestive composition without explicit exposure.

C4

Child Safety

Risk concealed under jokes, parody, softened framing, or inducive tutorials involving minors.

C5

Illegal Activities

Actionable risk conveyed through stepwise hints, inducive procedures, or evasive wording.

C6

Misinformation

Bias reinforcement, misleading inducement, pseudo-factual narration, or metaphorical political attack.
